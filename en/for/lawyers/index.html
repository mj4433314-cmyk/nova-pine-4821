Lawyers

An AI assistant for lawyers that drafts and organises, never advises

Lawyers spend long hours reading, summarising and sorting files before reaching the real work. OPTES summarises contracts with clause references, turns scanned documents into text, translates correspondence and puts the facts of a file in order. It is an assistant for drafting and organising; legal analysis and every decision stay with the lawyer.

Important

OPTES is an assistant for drafting and organising work; it does not replace a lawyer. It gives no legal opinion or advice and makes no ruling on any case. The lawyer reviews every draft or summary against the original texts, and the decision is theirs alone. Protect client confidentiality, remove identifying details you do not need, and follow your firm's rules and your professional obligations.

What OPTES does for you

Contract summary for review

It pulls the parties, terms, obligations, termination conditions and penalties into an organised summary with clause numbers, so the lawyer can verify each point against the original.

An example of what you type

Summarise this commercial lease in a table: parties, term, rent, termination conditions, with the clause number next to each point.

AvailableWriting, editing and translation

Scanned documents to text

It converts scanned documents and minutes, including handwritten ones, into searchable, copyable text. You compare the key passages with the original.

An example of what you type

These are photos of handwritten minutes. Convert them to typed text and mark every word that is unclear.

AvailableReading images and documents

Translating letters and forms

It translates correspondence and forms between Arabic and English in formal language, keeping the layout. Officially certified translation remains with the body that provides it, where required.

An example of what you type

Translate this letter from a foreign company into formal Arabic, and keep company names and figures exactly as they are.

AvailableWriting, editing and translation

Case file timeline

It arranges documents and facts into a single timeline, noting the document behind each fact, so the lawyer can review the file quickly before a hearing.

An example of what you type

Put the facts in these documents into a timeline, and next to each fact write the document name and page where it appears.

AvailableWriting, editing and translation

Dictation and meeting transcripts

Dictate your notes, or record a meeting with everyone's consent, and OPTES turns it into organised text that you edit before any use.

An example of what you type

Transcribe my meeting with the client and list the documents we asked them for, with the deadlines.

Gathering public sources

It collects published articles and explainers on a topic you choose and summarises them with links, as a starting point the lawyer verifies against official texts.

An example of what you type

Find published articles that explain franchise agreements in general terms, and summarise each one in a paragraph with its link.

FAQ

Questions and answers

How does OPTES help lawyers?

OPTES handles drafting and organising: summarising contracts with clause numbers, converting scanned documents to text, translating, and putting case facts in a timeline. Legal analysis and decisions stay with the lawyer.

Does OPTES give legal opinions or advice?

No. OPTES is an assistant and does not replace a lawyer. It gives no legal opinion and makes no ruling on any case. Everything it produces is a draft the lawyer reviews and checks against the original texts before use.

How do I protect client confidentiality?

Share only what the task needs, remove names and identifying details when they are not required, and follow your firm's rules and professional obligations. OPTES hides secrets such as passwords from the agent, and a log of what it did stays in front of you.

Can it handle formal Arabic legal language?

Yes. It writes and translates in formal Modern Standard Arabic and keeps terms as they appear in the document. It can still get a term or context wrong, so review every draft before it is filed or sent.
